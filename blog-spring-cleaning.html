Spring Cleaning Tips for Saskatchewan Homes

By Malinay Cleaning Team  ·  Regina, Saskatchewan  ·  April 2026

If you've lived in Saskatchewan long enough, you know that spring doesn't just bring warmer weather — it brings a whole season's worth of mess along with it. After months of snow, ice, road salt, and frozen everything, your home has taken a beating. The windows are grimy, the entryway is caked with dried mud, and somehow there's a layer of dust on surfaces you cleaned just two months ago.

Spring cleaning in Saskatchewan isn't just a nice-to-do. For most of us, it's practically a tradition — a deep exhale after a long, hard winter. And when you do it right, there's honestly nothing better than that feeling of walking into a home that is completely, thoroughly, deeply clean.

Start With the Entryway — It Took the Most Abuse

If there's one area of a Saskatchewan home that suffers the most through winter, it's the entryway. Boots caked in snow and salt, winter jackets piled up, sand and grit tracked in from every errand. It becomes the forgotten dumping ground from November to March.


Start here. Clear everything out — boots, shoes, jackets, scarves, bags — and give the space a proper reset. Wipe down walls and baseboards where salt residue tends to build up, wash or replace your entry mats, and give the floor a thorough mop. You'll be amazed how much lighter your whole home feels when the entryway is clean and organized.

Tackle Your Windows — Inside and Out

Saskatchewan winters are brutal on windows. Between the frost, the condensation, and the general grime of a cold season, most windows are practically opaque by the time spring rolls around. And once that prairie sunshine starts streaming in — it shows every single smudge.


Clean your windows inside and out. Use a streak-free glass cleaner or a simple mixture of warm water and white vinegar. Don't forget the window tracks and sills, which collect an impressive amount of dust, dead bugs, and debris over the winter months. While you're at it, wash your curtains and blinds — they absorb more dust than most people realize.

Deep Clean Your Floors

Winter floors in Saskatchewan go through a lot. Salt, sand, mud, and moisture get tracked in constantly, and even regular mopping can't always keep up. Spring is the time for a proper deep clean.


For hardwood and laminate floors, check for any salt residue that can dull the finish over time and use a hardwood-safe cleaner to restore the shine. For carpets, consider a deep steam clean — it pulls out the grime, allergens, and pet dander that have settled in over the winter. You'll notice the difference immediately, especially if anyone in your home has allergies.

Don't Forget Your Vents and Air Ducts

Here's one that often gets overlooked: your furnace vents and return air registers. After a full Saskatchewan winter of running the heat constantly, your vents are coated in dust — and every time the furnace kicks on, that dust gets circulated through your home.


Remove the vent covers and wash them thoroughly. Vacuum out as much of the duct as you can reach. And if it's been a few years, consider booking a professional duct cleaning — your lungs will thank you, especially as spring allergy season gets into full swing on the prairies.

Refresh Your Bedrooms and Closets

Spring is the perfect time to rotate your wardrobe and give your bedroom a proper reset. Pack away the heavy winter blankets, quilts, and flannel sheets — wash them before you store them so they're fresh and ready for next fall. Bring out your lighter spring bedding and give your mattress a good vacuum.


In your closets, take everything out, wipe down the shelves, and be intentional about what goes back in. Donate anything you haven't worn. Saskatchewan winters have a way of making us hold onto things we don't need — spring is a great time to let go.

Deep Clean Your Kitchen Appliances

We're not talking about a quick wipe of the counters. We mean the deep stuff — the inside of the oven, the range hood filter, the refrigerator coils at the back, the inside of the microwave, and yes, even behind and under the fridge and stove. These areas collect grease, dust, and crumbs all year long and a proper spring clean is the best time to address them.


A clean kitchen doesn't just look better — it runs more efficiently and can even extend the life of your appliances.

Open the Windows and Let Saskatchewan's Fresh Air In

Once that spring air finally warms up — and in Saskatchewan, we wait a long time for that moment — throw open your windows. Let the fresh prairie air move through your home. Air out your rugs, your cushions, your bedding. There is no cleaning product in the world that replaces the feeling of a home that has been properly aired out after a long winter.
